Joe was always a man of quiet routine. White dress shirt, beige cubicle, lunch at 12:30 sharp. He wasn't happy, exactly, but he had things in order. A steady job, a wife who once adored him, a son who still called him Dad, not Joe. But all of that begins to slip the moment a filthy, cackling stranger on the street locks eyes with him and says one word: Domino.

At first, it's easy to shrug off. Just a weird encounter. Nothing more. But then his computer crashes. He gets fired after two decades of loyal service, with no explanation and no severance. His mailbox breaks with the lightest of touches. A family of raccoons invades his front yard and topples his trash can for seven nights in a row. The Wi-Fi stops working. His son Isaac stops speaking to him. His wife Rachel starts calling him a loser to his face. And somehow, it all feels connected.

Now, as Joe sleepwalks through a crumbling life of dead houseplants, passive-aggressive Post-its, and existential HR voicemails, he embarks on an increasingly unhinged quest to make sense of the curse, or madness, that's consuming him. Is he being punished? Tested? Or is the world simply unraveling for no reason at all?

A modern, darkly comedic reimagining of the Book of Job, Domino is a bittersweet odyssey through spiritual decay, suburban absurdity, and one man's tragicomic fall from grace, one toppled piece at a time.
